# Study 117 · One shared write-ahead log per voter

2026-11-24 · Wave R4 · Baseline source revision `a02017d`

## Verdict

A voter can use one append-only WAL to cover independent tenant promises and accepts with one sync. Under the modeled media, quota, and key-custody contracts, projecting the WAL onto each group preserves the durable acceptor interface. A torn **uncovered** tail can discard work from several groups safely. A tear that removes a covered frame breaks the interface even when the other voting replicas still exist.

A single unindexed recovery scan that stops at corruption in any committed frame cannot supply per-tenant recovery isolation. The shared WAL needs independently trustworthy frame boundaries and scoped recovery, funded replacement space, and exact crash-selectable locators. A common device failure can take the voter out of service for all its groups and spends the same failure budget for each. It must never return a partial or blank acceptor under its old voting identity.

D02 stands. The WAL uses ciphertext boundaries from first persistence through every retained copy. At rotation, every selectable recovery path uses the clean successor and the new suffix generation. A hold retains its original basis, which becomes unreadable at the deadline. Ciphertext may survive rotation and deadline, and exact physical disposal is a later operation. The shared WAL preserves this contract only with tenant-scoped key custody and rotation evidence covering all possible recovery paths. The evidence does not qualify a production device or key authority. [Owner decisions](../requirements.md#owner-decisions--2026-11-23) · [Study 112](../112-erasure-rotation/report.md)

## Models and bounds

The same three TLC modules run with two layouts. **A** gives each group its own log and covering barrier. For the shipped mirrored voter this barrier represents completion of the locked two-slot update, including both syncs. **B** gives a voter one physical WAL sequence and a covering frontier. In a batch, advancing the frontier covers all complete frames admitted before it on that voter. Either layout replies separately for each exact group and ballot.

| Module | Bound and interface |
| --- | --- |
| [VoterWAL](VoterWAL.tla) | One voter, two groups, two ballots, one fixed exact prefix at each ballot, four frames per group, one outstanding request per group, one crash, and at most one scoped fault per group. Frame order, barrier, reply, and fault scheduling interleave. The modeled crash discards the entire uncovered suffix, including a possible tear across both groups. Extent authentication, framing, and the covered frontier are premises of this discrete model. Scoped corruption marks an existing durable group unreadable and quarantines it using its independently identified scope. Quota exhaustion refuses further writes after an allocation in that scope. Both preserve another group's recoverable and acknowledged image. |
| [WALAgreement](WALAgreement.tla) | Two groups, three voters, quorums `{1,2}` then `{2,3}`, one prepare and one accept at each of two ballots, one proposed extension per group, and an optional loss of AZ 1 after the first choice. There is one queued operation per voter and group in a wave. The prepared proposal is frozen from exact durable replies. The scheduler explores writes, barriers and replies inside each wave. The per-voter durable map abstracts the verified projection after sync. The second-loss control resets voter 2 under its existing identity. |
| [WALErasure](WALErasure.tla) | Two groups, two generations for the erasing tenant and one for the neighbour, one old record per tenant, an old ciphertext archive and an admitted view, one clean stage, publication, rotation, deadline, and compaction, one additional append per tenant, and at most one crash. A staged clean root is already durably backed. Every visible or durable selector must start at the clean root before commit. At the deadline the old key is unavailable to all modeled copies. The WAL rewrite abstracts a durably selected replacement. This model does not simulate key export, time, physical I/O, or a multi-voter erasure certificate. |

The proofs compose at the acceptor boundary. The voter model checks exact replies and reopen. The consensus model consumes those durable scoped images under the chosen schedule. The erasure model checks encryption and recovery selection independently of the consensus schedule. This is a modular design check. A concrete media and protocol refinement still has to prove that a recovered per-group projection admits the [DurableAcceptor](../lean/Logkeeper/DurableAcceptor.lean) traces and fixed-group ranked agreement of Study 27, and that selection, quorum changes, and key destruction compose.

[SharedWAL.lean](SharedWAL.lean) proves projection over arbitrary concatenated logs, isolation from other-scope appends, retention of an exact replied frame across recovery from a protected prefix plus any valid tail, coverage of all written frames on a sync, and exact scoped cleanup. Its witness syncs frames from two groups and reopens their exact projections after discarding two later pending frames. The proof assumes the exact covered bytes survive and keeps decode, crypto, the file protocol, and voter repair outside the theorem. Eight theorem axiom reports use only standard Lean axioms.

## Syncs and replication

Count one completed logical barrier for an acceptor update, then account for the actual disk calls separately. Let `d` be the number of committed decisions, `P_v` and `A_v` the promises and accepts persisted by voter `v`, and `b_v` its nonempty covering batches for those updates. For this comparison, include durable work from slow participants or retries in the numerator when it occurs during the counted workload.

| Layout | Logical syncs per decision over a run | Balanced batches with `k` groups per voter in each phase and `q` voters participating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A · Group-private | `sum_v(P_v + A_v) / d` | `2q` with prepare plus accept, `q` for the accept phase alone |
| B · Voter-shared | `sum_v b_v / d` | `2q/k` with prepare plus accept, `q/k` for the accept phase alone |

At quorum size 2 the current source path uses prepare and accept for each choice, so it needs at least four logical barriers per choice. Each group-private mirrored update calls `sync_all` twice, for at least **eight actual file syncs** per choice. Preparations on a third voter, retries, setup, compaction, and rotation readback add work. A single qualified WAL sync per voter-phase batch would use **`4/k` actual data syncs** per choice in the balanced case. If the WAL needs a second separately durable frontier or mirror barrier per batch, count it too. For a prepare-free accept phase the figures are **four** mirrored file syncs and **`2/k`** single-barrier WAL syncs at quorum size 2. The modeled two-group, two-ballot path commits four decisions with 16 logical barriers in A and 8 when B has full batches. For sparse arrivals `k=1`. Only co-located groups with pending work on the same voter can share a sync. The counts exclude internal provider I/O and give no D06 latency result. [Choice and confirmation](../../src/ordered/replication.rs) · [Mirrored media adapter](../verus/mirrored_voter_media/README.md)

| Medium | Coverage at a two-vote decision | With all three voters caught up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regional EFS plus mirrored slots | Two independent logical votes, four application slot images | Three logical voter images, six application slot images |
| Regional EFS plus single shared WAL | Two independent logical votes, two application WAL images | Three logical voter images, three application WAL images |
| Local NVMe plus single shared WAL | Two independent AZ-local voter images | Three independent AZ-local voter images |

The count measures representations of the decision. Within a voter the two slots can share a failure domain. Regional EFS further stores the file bytes redundantly across AZs. If a simplified uniform full-copy model uses `r` backend copies per application image, the first two rows scale by `r` and the local row has one storage image per voter. The cited AWS EFS specification describes regional redundancy and gives no exact full-copy factor for this calculation. Provider redundancy adds no logical votes. Group WAL sync and provider replication are independent design choices. S3 payloads, checkpoints and other referenced data retain separate coverage and erasure obligations. [AWS EFS Regional durability](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/efs/latest/ug/features.html) · [Protection interval](../105-service-scope-protection/report.md)

## L10, H/G3, and local media

- **L10.** Keep the exact reply floor and ranked recovery interface. Replace the two-file theorem with a per-group WAL projection theorem over every crash-selectable manifest, starting from exact durable byte and locator evidence. The existing L10 result tolerates one detectable slot tear under its sync premise. A naked single WAL that can tear already-acknowledged data loses that guarantee. Either qualify immutable covered regions at the chosen fault boundary or supply independent exact voter-local coverage and prove repair or fallback under that tear. A peer majority is a different failure boundary. [SelectionFence](../lean/Logkeeper/SelectionFence.lean) · [L10 shipped correspondence](../verus/mirrored_voter_media/README.md)
- **G3 media and H codec.** The inventory places filesystem crash modeling in G3 and whole-message codecs and decoded chosen-history refinement in H. Model crash cuts through batch write, data and locator persistence, reply, warm reopen and subsequent power loss, selection, rewrite, and reclamation. Exercise multiple trailing partial frames, a tear across the last covered and first uncovered frame, ambiguous sync failure, and damaged length or index metadata. The codec must bind the voter and group incarnation, generation, frame identity and order, exact promise and accepted rank, bounds and bytes, and any referenced image to its digest. A valid-but-uncovered frame is retained only with qualified revalidation and durability and does not certify a group decision. Repair or quarantine needs authenticated exact frame boundaries independent of corrupted tenant bytes. [Formal inventory](../formal-inventory.md) · [Media and reopen studies](../26-journal-rotation/report.md)
- **Rotation evidence.** L13 and A495, A502, and A511 currently use exact mirrored readback. Prove and bind replacement quorum evidence that every crash-selectable path protects the clean checkpoint and fenced suffix. Keep old, staged, archived, repaired, and held ciphertext within the tenant's revocation domain, including cached key material, aliases, delayed decryptions and pending producer I/O. Compaction preserves other tenant frames and uses funded workspace while any old path or borrow is still live. [Prior amendments](../amendments.md)
- **Local NVMe.** With one durable voter in each of three independent AZs, a two-voter decision has at least one surviving exact copy after one loss. A surviving recovery majority intersects it and recovers the decision if its overlap voter keeps the promise and accepted rank. A replacement with blank instance-store storage must be fenced until exact installed repair. Renew the interval only with verified coverage and a ready recovery quorum. D03 still forbids one-copy acknowledgment during unclassified loss. The blank-overlap control loses AZ 1 and then returns voter 2 empty under its old identity. The new majority chooses a conflicting prefix. This is outside the single-failure premise and shows why EFS-to-NVMe needs an explicit host/disk loss and replacement policy. EC2 instance store survives ordinary reboot and may be erased by stop, termination, or disk failure. If the permitted envelope combines an AZ loss with loss of the surviving overlap voter's covered bytes, majority-only local storage is insufficient. A second loss before repair, correlated faults, and recovery across arbitrary host replacement need stronger backing or a revised fault envelope. [AWS EC2 instance-store lifetime](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/instance-store-lifetime.html) · [Study 105](../105-service-scope-protection/report.md)

Per-tenant order, exact reply floors, scoped recovery and resource accounting, certified one-AZ protection, revocation isolation, and exact data dependencies are essential. Per-group files, mirroring as a representation, the number of filesystem barriers, and EFS as the medium are choices under those contracts. B is a candidate reduction in syncs. A shared flush queue, recovery index, and compaction workspace are additional mechanisms to justify against measured workloads. Open qualification work includes the authenticated frame and index codec, actual persistence and namespace semantics on both media, proof of concurrent compaction and cohort repair with pending batches and holds, deadline enforcement across custody locations, and cold recovery, tail latency and fairness under load.
