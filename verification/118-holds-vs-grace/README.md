# Study 118 · Exact holds and reachability with a grace period

Wave R5 · Logkeeper and RTDB Multi · 2026-11-24

**Keep exact protection for an admitted basis and exact provider authority for physical disposition.** Reachability with a grace period can support restartable work and opportunistic ciphertext collection. A slow analytic read, a fork promised at a specific cut, a detached provider call, and a deadline-bound key do not share a lifetime. A single ordered pin can replace the read-side hold variants for the tested contract, subject to the lifetime and admission rules below.

[Prior work and amendment ledger](prior-work.md) traces the existing custody requirements. [Expected results](expected-results.txt) records the checked cases and their outcomes.

B's passing configuration requires two pieces of exact state even outside per-reader pins. First, the collection decision and new publication must arbitrate over the same complete reachability graph. A generation already handed to an asynchronous delete must be ineligible for new publication until a freshly backed identity is registered. Physical separates authorization and deletion, and the stale-mark control permits registration between them. An atomic collector/provider effect or recoverable exclusion through the effect could realize this premise. A mark followed by a recheck and an asynchronous delete leaves a race. Second, each effect must identify its provider version and custody scope. A checksum authenticates payload content and an allocation identity scopes a side effect.

## Quantitative premises

All times below are true elapsed times unless identified as clock settings. At a zero-reference mark at time `r`, the saved timestamp is `r + e₀`; later the collector tests `t + e₁ >= r + e₀ + G`. Each sample error lies in `[-ε,+ε]`. For independent extremes, the earliest permitted collection is at age `G − 2ε` (clamped to age zero) and by age `G + 2ε` the time test always passes. Provider, queue and scan time can delay actual collection further. A shared clock with a proven bound on elapsed-time error can use that bound in place of `2ε`.

| Property | Exact premise in the model and scope in the product |
| --- | --- |
| **No wrong data** | No timing inequality is needed for fail-closed reads. Bind the lineage and certified cut, verify the exact root and typed suffix against it, and validate each immutable payload. A missing or mismatched input refuses. Only a fresh latest invocation or recovery attempt can capture a newer target, and its complete result must come from that target. Recovery still has to cover the required committed prefix. An already named or exposed basis stays fixed. Retargeting the answer and trusting latest-by-key both fail. |
| **No reachable version collected** | No timing inequality replaces complete roots and exact arbitration. Count every selectable recovery root, pending publication, durable fork, typed suffix, and any admitted cut promised continued use. The modeled B can protect the listed durable roots. It does not see a promised cut that exists solely as a node-local pin. A new root and withdrawal must serialize against the same version. A fixed grace only protects invisible work whose last physical use is known to precede its earliest possible collection. |
| **Stable absence and bounded detached I/O** | With old admission effectively closed by `r` and a hard bound `D` on all old effects, including the endpoint `r + D`, the sharp condition is **`G > D + 2ε`**. Equality permits delete then create or delete before a detached completion in the same tick. If the last effect can be issued `H` after the mark, replace `D` by `H + D`, or use the effective admission-close horizon as the timestamp origin. Physical checks a worker that can still admit at tick 2 with a slow clock, with completion at tick 4. For `ε=1`, `G=6` fails and `G=7` passes. A client timeout, request issue time, or lease expiry is not evidence for `D`. A bounded `D` must include retries, work accepted before a lost reply, staging, and backend effects. |
| **Fresh read and recovery progress** | Let `C` be a lower bound on the duration of a usable certified basis before overtaking trim. Let `O` bound discovery/admission after its publication and `W` bound one whole attempt, including replay, queueing and all provider operations. With bounded re-alignment on a newer publication and stable input access, strict restart is guaranteed in the periodic schedule exactly when **`O + W < C`**. At equality trim can win the tie. A latest checkpoint frequency target or average cadence gives no lower bound. More generally, a fresh attempt needs at least one adequately serviced window of remaining lifetime exceeding its work bound. Arbitrary phase or weak fairness alone does not supply that window. |
| **Continued use of an admitted old cut** | No finite `G` covers unbounded old use invisible to the collector. A different contract that permits finishing over trim can let bounded hidden work continue only if *every* required input, including journal suffix, remains usable through grace. With capture no later than the trim and no new acquisition after it, a full post-trim remaining-work bound `W` gives `G > W + 2ε`. For an aligned attempt on a freshly published basis with a guaranteed `C` before trim, the earliest cleanup window is at least `C + G − 2ε`; finishing by `O+W` fits strictly inside it when the grace window is positive. An initial stale selection or later opportunistic admission needs its additional horizon included. These are timing consequences of the Lean bounds, outside strict-restart B. In particular, S3 grace does not preserve a trimmed journal suffix. |
| **D02 erasure** | `G`, `D` and ordinary checkpoint cadence alone cannot make retained ciphertext, detached plaintext output, or an escaped key unreadable. The commit must fence key admission, order a clean recoverable successor for every selector, fence old-generation appends, and irrevocably arm revocation. Old-held outputs must check the authority at delivery or have an equivalent effective boundary. An inaccessible provider may delay ciphertext cleanup under L03. |

On the strict-restart branch the progress inequality and physical grace inequality constrain different events. Once trim invalidates the accessible replay basis, increasing object grace cannot repair the lasso. If work finishes earlier than its bound or a schedule has an unusually long window, a particular execution may succeed despite a failing worst-case bound. `W` and `O` are elapsed-time budgets. Any configured clock-derived budget must account for its clock error before using the inequality. Admission slots, a reachable installed quorum, certified exact inputs and their required AZ coverage remain premises.

For D02, let `δ` be the true interval from ordered erasure commit to the deadline and `A` the local revocation threshold elapsed from the arming sample. With the same independent sample errors, expiry by the deadline requires `A + 2ε <= δ`. To promise readability throughout **every** instant before `δ`, the earliest expiry would have to be no earlier than `δ`, requiring `A − 2ε >= δ`. A positive uncertainty bound makes both promises impossible with this single threshold. With an exact authoritative time boundary the model preserves the full window and uses the erased result at and after the deadline. A conservative threshold meets the deadline but guarantees old readability only before `A − 2ε`, with other inputs still available. A threshold of `δ − 2ε` leaves up to `4ε` of early cutoff in this model. If that early cutoff is accepted, record it explicitly as a clarification or relaxation of the full predeadline view promise in A463 and R7. This result applies equally to A and B. The qualified authority may realize a single exact contract boundary or expose a deliberately conservative window. Preparation and provider reconciliation occur before the commit and can take longer than the deadline interval. ## Minimum read-side primitive

`pin(position, until)` is an ordered compare-and-reserve of an exact certified cut, returning an authenticated root and protected nonempty suffix as one admitted basis. Its scope includes lineage, owner and a non-reused pin identity. A fresh latest read selects the current admissible position in that operation. A request for a named cut either acquires that cut while still available or refuses at admission. Pin publication and the retention/withdrawal decision must serialize.

Count the pin's transitive exact carrier closure as a root. Allow renewal only before expiry with current authorization, optionally allow authenticated early release, and make expiry stop new use without losing custody for provider work already admitted. An elapsed-time implementation requiring every use to finish within `W` needs earliest effective expiry strictly after `W`. If two independent clock samples govern expiry, configure the length greater than `W + 2ε` or provide a per-effect drain beyond logical expiry. A fork either publishes its durable reference in the same exclusion domain before releasing the temporary pin, or publishes independently verified backing. Pins must survive handoff and every admitted recovery selector. If a long operation must survive loss of the renewal path, its initial reservation must cover that interval or survive until release or effective stop. A standing pin can cover several local reads if each is admitted against exactly its certified scope and the last admitted physical operation is accounted for.

Cuts and Cadence establish the basis and progress result with this single read-side primitive. Physical shows why it is insufficient to fence a delayed write, and Erasure shows why key custody stays separate. Exact current-grant validation, transitive dependency enumeration, fault-envelope coverage, original version authorization and stable provider disposition can be modularized at their respective owners. The command names, per-operation mark representation, and optional early-release round trip are implementation choices. The exact obligations remain.

## Requirements and owner decisions

- **D01** stays. A lost read reply is a fresh admission at the same logical basis. A latest retry within an unfinished invocation may choose a newer point only under the latest-read contract. Permitting a caller's named retry or fork to move to a newer basis would change D01's basis rule and S79. Returning an explicit unavailable or retained-floor refusal preserves value correctness but weakens S06, S11, S12, S23, P12, P15 and P16 for already admitted work. Mutation receipts remain exact.
- **D02** stays if the key protocol and deadline hold. Grace-only erasure or early revocation of a full-window held view would change the accepted erasure behavior. The early-window alternative needs an explicit owner interpretation of A463 and the R7 view promise.
- **D03** stays. The model takes the certified fault envelope as a premise. Removing coverage or counting a withdrawing version toward it would weaken S02 and S89–S95, regardless of delay bounds.
- **D04** stays. Watching from a certified replacement after cursor expiry is already restartable. An admitted exact view, retained retry, or fork has its own basis.
- **D05** stays. The claims concern installed journals and scoped provider paths. Directory and legacy users do not supply the required proofs.
- **D06** stays. `D`, `ε`, read and recovery work, observation lag, checkpoint timing, and retention overlap need qualification at the implemented system. No timings here are production measurements. Making bounded provider delay a product assumption in place of exact tickets and evidence would amend S08, S16–S17, S31–S33, S43, S51, S60 and S92–S93, and would remove L17's progress path when an admitted request never completes.

**Verdict.** B is sound for fail-closed restartable operations given authenticated values, exact current/durable reachability arbitration, and the stated service and delay premises. It is unsound as a replacement for all admitted-view and physical custody guarantees. Use a pin for an admitted basis and a generation-scoped provider effect boundary or verified drain for original physical obligations. Keep D02's independent key authority.

## Representative TLC counterexamples

| Case | Shortest trace or lasso |
| --- | --- |
| A drops the provider fence, `StableAbsence` | Queue write, retire the writer, authorize withdrawal, delete version 1, then deliver the conditional create. Five transitions. |
| B uses `G=4, D=2, ε=1`, `StableAbsence` | Queue write and mark with clock error −1. At tick 2 the check with error +1 authorizes collection. Delete and complete the old write at the same true tick. Seven transitions. The pin-only writer control has the same trace. |
| B misses the worker's last admission, `BorrowBacked` | Retire at tick 0. A slow worker admits at tick 2. With `G=6, D=2, ε=1` the collector deletes at tick 4 with that operation outstanding. Eight transitions. |
| A or B lets a stale mark authorize deletion, `LiveRootsBacked` | Authorize removal as unreachable, register a new root for the still-present version, then delete. Four transitions for A and seven for B. Ignoring any one of the configured exact root roles also fails. |
| A or B deletes by the latest version or uses a marker | Deleting the replacement violates `LiveRootsBacked`. A key-only absence based on a delete marker lets settlement certify while version 1 persists, violating `ExactSettlement`. |
| B loses a named cut, `RequestedBasisBacked` | Capture cut 0, publish cut 1, trim the old suffix. Three transitions. Explicit refusal at the next step breaks `ExactNamedService` for the admitted basis but satisfies `NoWrongData`. |
| B does not reread root or silently retargets, `RetryRereadsRoot` / `NoWrongData` | Start at cut 0, publish 1, trim the suffix, then either retry using the stale basis or return cut 1 under cut 0's invocation. Four transitions. |
| B reaches successive trim boundaries, `EventuallyServed` | At `O=1,W=2,C=3`, admit one tick after publication, reach work age 2 at the next trim, refuse the stale attempt, and re-admit one tick into the next cycle. Every action runs fairly. The recovery case repeats with `W=3,C=4`. |
| Grace-only erasure, `DeadlineUnreadable` | Stage both clean slots and commit. At tick 6 the old key still opens retained ciphertext. Nine transitions. |
| Drift versus predeadline readability, `FullViewWindow` | Arm at commit with error −1, test with error +1 at tick 2, and expire four ticks before the deadline. Arming the full six ticks instead permits a still-live key at tick 6. |

## Reproduce and interpret the checks

`python3 verification/118-holds-vs-grace/run.py` uses Lean 4.35.0 via `~/.elan/bin/lake` and the pinned TLA+ tools jar version 1.7.4, TLC 2.19. The runner finds the study's JDK and jar under `~/cycle3` when present. `JAVA_BIN`, `LOGKEEPER_TLA_JAR` and `LAKE_BIN` can select local tools. The shared runner checks the jar hash and validates the exact invariant name or the sole configured temporal property. A witness is checked by deliberately negating its target condition and demanding a reachable violation. Logs and generated configurations are under `.results/`. The four named suites are `physical`, `cuts`, `erasure` and `lean`.

| Model | TLC bounds |
| --- | --- |
| Physical | One retiring version, at most one queued writer operation and one independent borrow per scenario, one worker, two total physical versions, one original root/referrer role selected per scenario and at most one racing root acquisition. Time ticks 0–10, drift 0 or 1, positive grace 1–7, bounded effect delay 2 or unbounded within the horizon. Lease end is tick 2. The worker progress case is weakly fair, including time and provider completion. An unbounded-delay case demonstrates a late effect within the finite horizon and makes no finite-time service claim. |
| Cuts | Two distinguishable certified cuts, one invocation, at most one fresh recapture, two independent old inputs and at most one durable fork transfer. Untimed model. An object-collection action represents an interval after its grace has elapsed. |
| Cadence | One operation, periodic trims at 2–5 ticks, observation lag 1, work 2 or 3 ticks, pin lifetime 2–4 where tested. Time phase wraps while invalidation remains sticky for each attempt. Weak fairness of time, admission and finish, with forced service by the work bound, still permits the TLC retry lassos. |
| Erasure | One rotation and erasure, two crash-selectable slots, two generations, one pending old-view open and one queued key-copy operation. Deadline tick 6, horizon 8, drift 0 or 1, arm interval 4 or 6. Finite expiry and postdeadline recovery are checked with weak fairness. Ciphertext stays retained. |

[Bounds.lean](Bounds.lean) proves the strict grace exclusion and its tight counterexample, the pinned-closure deletion law for arbitrary version sets, the aligned-attempt window, the latest safe key expiry, and incompatibility of an exact predeadline window with a single two-sample threshold under nonzero integer uncertainty. Controls weaken the grace inequality, omit pin reachability, allow the trim tie, and remove the arming margin. All six theorems use standard axioms only.

The models isolate failure mechanisms and do not implement a journal, hash, provider, clock or key service. They assume genuine certified cuts and exact digest verification. The fork transfer is atomic, the physical version scope is authenticated, and erasure considers a scoped old key domain. Consensus, actual replay decoding, independent export, complete staging accounting, cross-AZ topology and real restart/cache proofs need their existing composition or qualification evidence.
